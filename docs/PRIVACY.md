# Unearth Privacy Policy

**Last updated:** 13 October 2026

Unearth (“we”, “the app”) helps people share little-known public places on a map. This policy explains what we collect and why.

## What we collect

- **Account:** Sign in with Apple identity (Apple user id), optional email/name Apple provides, and the handle you choose.
- **Location (when you allow it):** Your device location while you use Dig, so nearby gems and distances can be shown. We request **When In Use** access only — we do not track you in the background.
- **Content you post:** Gem names, categories, map pins, descriptions, liked/didn’t-like notes, tags, optional Google Maps links, and stash saves.
- **Reports:** If you report a gem or find, we store the report reason and related ids so we can review it.
- **Technical:** Authentication tokens needed to keep you signed in.

## How we use it

- To run Dig, Unearth, Stash, Activity, and Profile.
- To show your finds and Prospectors level to you (and public handle/finds where the product exposes them).
- To enforce posting guidelines and handle abuse reports.
- To revoke Sign in with Apple access if you delete your account.

## Sharing

We do not sell your personal data. Content you Unearth is shared with other Unearth users as part of the product. We use infrastructure providers (e.g. hosting and database) solely to operate the service.

## Retention & deletion

You can delete your account in Profile → Delete account. That removes your user record and associated content we store, and revokes Apple token access where possible.

## Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue on [github.com/RyanBantu/unearth](https://github.com/RyanBantu/unearth) or contact the developer listed on the App Store listing.
